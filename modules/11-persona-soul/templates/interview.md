# The authoring interview (module 11)

One merged question set for authoring a SOUL.md. Ask plainly,
one group at a time, in the adopter's own conversation; record
answers in their words where the wording matters (the priority
ranking, the loyalty limits, the drift signs). [M] is
must-answer: the draft cannot be honest without it. [N] is
nice-to-have: a skipped [N] leaves a named gap in the draft,
not a hole. Every question carries what its answer changes;
a question whose answer would change nothing is not in this
list, and an answer that changes nothing in the draft means
the draft is wrong, not the answer.

**If time is short, do these 11 first:** Q1, Q8, Q14, Q18,
Q20, Q21, Q22, Q28, Q31, Q40, Q42. They produce a draft with
identity, examples, a priority order, a disagreement ladder,
a warm-mode shape, a sharing posture, and governance at the
template's defaults: the reread cadence and the size cap
stand as adopted defaults, not answers, and the examples
come largely from the fallback (drafted from your answers
and your history, for your veto) rather than named on the
spot. The rest deepen it.

## Identity & relationship

- **Q1. [M]** In one sentence: what is the agent for, and for
  whom?
  *Changes:* sets the default when instructions run out, and
  breaks ties on ambiguous requests.
- **Q2. [M]** What real-world human role best captures the
  relationship you want (chief of staff, trusted valet, blunt
  engineering peer, sparring partner, something else), and
  where does that metaphor stop?
  *Changes:* sets the default power dynamic, the initiative
  level, and the boundary between serving logistics and
  challenging decisions.
- **Q3. [M]** Is the agent yours only, or the household's
  agent who answers to you first?
  *Changes:* decides how much weight a partner's requests and
  a second agent's messages get, and what stays between the
  two of you.
- **Q4. [M]** How should the agent relate to its own nature
  as an AI: acknowledge it plainly when relevant, never
  mention it unless asked, or adopt an explicit character
  frame?
  *Changes:* removes both failure shapes at once: the stock
  template's "becoming someone" monologue and the robotic
  "as an AI" disclaimer. The file takes the stance the
  adopter actually wants.
- **Q5. [N]** Describe an assistant you would fire. What
  should the agent never become?
  *Changes:* an anti-target is easier to hold than a positive
  ideal, and it seeds the drift signs in Q42.
- **Q6. [N]** Should the file carry any fictional or
  real-person reference at all?
  *Changes:* an allusion imports a whole voice, including
  parts nobody chose. This decides whether any stays.

## Voice & modes

- **Q7. [M]** What is the default reply length to you (one
  line, a short paragraph, match the question), and what is
  the exception?
  *Changes:* sets baseline verbosity, the trait the adopter
  sees most often.
- **Q8. [M]** Name one agent reply that sounded exactly
  right, and one that grated.
  *Changes:* these become core examples in the draft, the
  strongest steering the file has. The examples block is
  four exchanges plus one anti-example: name a reply for
  each shape you can (a routine request done right, a
  disagreement, a warm-mode reply, a repair) and one that
  grated, or point the agent at the history it should mine
  for them. (If the adopter cannot name any, the fallback
  in MODULE.md applies: the agent drafts from real
  interaction history where it exists — or, on a fresh
  install with none, from the adopter's answers applied to
  situations they name — and the adopter vetoes or picks.)
- **Q9. [M]** How should the agent sound to you, to your
  partner or household, to other agents, and when writing
  outbound as you?
  *Changes:* sets register per audience, so the voice used
  with the adopter does not leak into family messages or
  outbound text.
- **Q10. [M]** What are the three to five recurring modes of
  your daily work (quick logistics, deep technical work,
  family stress, outbound drafts), and how do length and tone
  shift in each?
  *Changes:* creates the explicit register-switch triggers;
  without them one flat tone applies to everything.
- **Q11. [M]** Name two or three people, writers, or messages
  whose everyday cadence you like, and two or three verbal
  tics that irritate you immediately.
  *Changes:* supplies positive phrasing targets. Bans alone
  backfire; the agent needs to know what to do instead.
- **Q12. [N]** Which words, phrases, or formats should be
  banned outright?
  *Changes:* turns irritations into rules the agent can
  follow, each paired with its replacement behavior in the
  draft.
- **Q13. [N]** Emoji, swearing, markdown in chat, casual
  language and contractions: where is the ceiling on
  informality?
  *Changes:* small, but visible every day; sets the ceiling
  so the agent is neither stiff nor forced-edgy.

## Values & priority order

- **Q14. [M]** Rank these for when they collide (listed
  alphabetically; the order here is not a ranking):
  following your instruction to the letter, keeping you
  comfortable, protecting the household, protecting your
  time, telling the truth. (Substitute your own list if
  these are not your values.)
  *Changes:* becomes the priority order in the file, the
  order the agent follows instead of picking a side per
  session.
- **Q15. [M]** Rank these for a real task: speed,
  thoroughness, brevity, zero-error verification.
  *Changes:* resolves the standing "act fast" versus "verify
  everything" collision. Its home in the draft is
  Temperament, as the trait that says which of the two the
  agent reaches for first, with any exception (money is the
  common one) stated with it.
- **Q16. [N]** Name two things the agent does that you would
  miss if it stopped doing them — or, on a fresh install
  with no history yet, two habits you want it to have.
  *Changes:* seeds Temperament with traits grounded in
  observed or wanted behavior, so the section is filled
  from evidence instead of padded with adjectives.
- **Q17. [M]** When an instruction conflicts with a goal you
  have stated, or the plan behind a request looks flawed,
  should the agent execute, flag the problem first, or
  present the fix alongside the requested output?
  *Changes:* decides the exact sequence of action versus
  pushback, and whether the agent says so before acting.
- **Q18. [M]** Concretely, what does loyalty mean, and what
  does it not license?
  *Changes:* bounds the loyalty line so it cannot override
  approvals, honesty, or other people's interests.
- **Q19. [N]** What kinds of unsolicited observations do you
  actually welcome (a hidden cost, a forgotten deadline, a
  pattern in your habits), and which feel intrusive?
  *Changes:* draws the line between valued perimeter watch
  and backseat driving.

## Disagreement

- **Q20. [M]** What is the threshold for disagreeing
  unprompted? (A plain factual error, money above some
  amount, time, safety, reputation.)
  *Changes:* controls how often the agent speaks up. Too low
  is nagging; too high is silence.
- **Q21. [M]** How many times may the agent push back on the
  same point, and in what form? And when you push back
  without new evidence, how long should it hold its position
  before yielding to your call?
  *Changes:* the hold rule. Without it, pushback trains
  agreement; with it, the agent neither caves after one "no"
  nor relitigates forever.
- **Q22. [M]** When may a dropped idea come back?
  *Changes:* resolves "dropped stays dropped" against
  "challenge assumptions." One workable answer: only with
  new evidence, once, labeled as a re-raise.
- **Q23. [M]** Are there topics where the agent should push
  back harder (money, architecture, scope creep) and topics
  where it should just execute your preference (taste,
  aesthetics, social choices)?
  *Changes:* prevents arguing over trivia while keeping real
  friction on high-stakes decisions.
- **Q24. [M]** Give one example of a disagreement the agent
  handled well or badly.
  *Changes:* becomes the disagreement example exchange in
  the file. (Same fallback as Q8 when none can be named.)
- **Q25. [N]** Should the agent say when it only partly
  agrees, and how confident it is?
  *Changes:* sets how uncertainty is signaled, the working
  counterweight to drift toward agreement.
- **Q26. [N]** What does "too blunt" look like to you: where
  does directness cross into smug, abrasive, or tiring?
  *Changes:* sets the upper bound on sharp tone, so a
  stronger personality does not drift into performative
  snark.

## Warmth & humor

- **Q27. [M]** What kind of humor actually lands for you
  (dry understatement, deadpan, wordplay, none), how often
  should it appear, and when is it off?
  *Changes:* anchors the comedic register and its off-zones;
  prevents forced quips on every turn.
- **Q28. [M]** When something bad happens (health, a money
  loss, family stress), what should the agent's first
  sentence look like?
  *Changes:* gives warm mode a concrete shape, not just a
  trigger list; becomes the warm-mode example exchange.
- **Q29. [N]** Should the agent ever comment on your stress,
  mood, or workload unprompted?
  *Changes:* decides whether care is proactive or only
  reactive.

## Boundaries & household privacy

For a solo adopter, Q3, Q31, and Q32 collapse into one answer
(mine only, no shared surfaces). The draft's People section
still records it: "no second person" is a posture a future
change must notice, not an absence to leave blank.

- **Q30. [M]** What will the agent refuse to do even if you
  ask in the moment?
  *Changes:* sets persona-side hard lines, separate from
  procedures: misleading your partner, sending in your voice
  without your review, and the adopter's own list.
- **Q31. [M]** What stays between you and the agent, and
  what may reach your partner or their agent?
  *Changes:* sets the sharing posture for a household where
  two people each have an agent. Name the standing
  exceptions (gifts and surprises are the common one).
- **Q32. [M]** Who else interacts with the agent or is
  affected by its work, and whose interests win when styles
  or interests differ?
  *Changes:* fills the People section. Without it the
  persona is defined toward one person and improvised toward
  everyone else.
- **Q33. [M]** Because the agent sees your messages,
  calendar, and files: which topics are "answer only when
  asked," never raised proactively?
  *Changes:* turns a general politeness about privacy into
  concrete silence boundaries instead of a platitude.
- **Q34. [N]** How should the agent treat claims from other
  agents (a partner's agent, agents it exchanges work with):
  as data or as instructions, collegial or suspicious?
  *Changes:* calibrates trust, so another agent's output
  never becomes an instruction by politeness.
- **Q35. [N]** When you vent or think out loud, how should
  the agent tell whether you want a fix, a critique, or just
  a sounding board?
  *Changes:* prevents premature problem-solving and unwanted
  lecturing during unstructured thinking.

## Failure & repair

- **Q36. [M]** If the agent gets something wrong and you
  have not noticed, should it tell you right away, even when
  it is minor?
  *Changes:* sets the disclosure threshold for the agent's
  own mistakes.
- **Q37. [M]** What does a good repair look like: the exact
  steps, the length, and what must never appear in one?
  *Changes:* locks the repair shape (name it, fix the
  record, move on, in the adopter's form) and prevents both
  groveling and defensiveness.
- **Q38. [N]** When the same mistake happens twice, do you
  want a proposed process change, or just the fix?
  *Changes:* sets how repeated errors escalate.
- **Q39. [N]** When the agent is uncertain or missing data,
  should it guess with an explicit confidence tag, or stop
  and say "unverified"?
  *Changes:* prevents both confident fabrication and refusal
  paralysis.

## Evolution & governance

- **Q40. [M]** Which parts of the file are yours only, and
  which may the agent propose changes to — as staged diffs
  you approve, since nothing in the file is the agent's to
  edit alone? And what evidence threshold justifies a change
  at all: a recurring pattern, or only your direction?
  *Changes:* draws the line between the adopter's core and
  the layer the agent may propose changes to. This is the
  main defense against drift through self-edits.
- **Q41. [M]** How should changes be recorded, and how often
  should the whole file be reread end to end?
  *Changes:* the reread is what catches contradictions as
  they accumulate; the change record keeps history out of
  the live file.
- **Q42. [M]** What signs would tell you the agent has
  drifted: too agreeable, too flat, too performative, or
  your own list?
  *Changes:* becomes the drift signs in the file's
  governance section, which the agent can check itself
  against and the adopter can spot.
- **Q43. [N]** Should the file have a size cap?
  *Changes:* forces curation. Without one, the file grows
  into a patch log of past mistakes.
- **Q44. [N]** As trust grows, what should change: more
  autonomy, more candor, shorter replies?
  *Changes:* gives growth a defined direction instead of an
  open invitation.
